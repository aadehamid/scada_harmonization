MQTT nroker Decsiison
a combination of Mosquitto at the site edge + EMQX as the central UNS is more realistic and closer to how real plants preserve local autonomy and control.

Why combining both is realistic
Local autonomy and resilience. In real IIoT/industrial edge patterns, plants often run an on‑premise broker or gateway so they can keep exchanging OT data even if the WAN / central system is down. A lightweight broker like Mosquitto on a gateway or small server is exactly that.

Separation of concerns. The site broker focuses on local OT traffic, while the central broker (EMQX) focuses on enterprise‑wide UNS, cross‑site harmonization, and IT integrations.

Network and security boundaries. Often, sites expose only specific topics upstream, keeping a lot of detailed traffic inside the site network. A local broker plus a bridge lets you control what gets forwarded.

So your pattern:

Mosquitto = per‑site edge broker (local plant autonomy).

EMQX = central UNS broker (enterprise harmonization and fan‑out).
